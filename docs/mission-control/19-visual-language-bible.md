# 1. Core Visual Philosophy

A website must do two jobs at the same time:

1. Make the business look more expensive, trustworthy, and modern.
2. Push the visitor toward a call, quote request, booking, or lead form.

Beautiful design that does not convert is decoration.
Conversion design that looks cheap is not enough.
Mission Control combines both.

The visual standard:

- Premium
- Clean
- Modern
- Fast
- Mobile-first
- Trust-building
- Industry-specific
- Conversion-focused
- Distinctive
- Cinematic where appropriate
- Not generic AI slop

Every website should feel like the business was upgraded from "old local company site" to "serious modern brand."

---

# 2. Mission Control's Visual Positioning

Mission Control is not trying to be PeachWeb, Spline, Unicorn, Dora, Vectary, Webflow, Genspark, Rosebud, or SEELE.

Mission Control extracts their strongest patterns and aims them at a more practical money lane:

> Outdated local business websites that need premium rebuilds, better trust, better mobile design, better CTA placement, and stronger lead generation.

The goal is not to sell "3D websites" first.
The goal is to sell premium website upgrades that make local businesses look worth calling.

The 3D, motion, cinematic visuals, and AI-generated hero art are the sauce.
The core product is a better business website.

# 3. Reverse-Engineered Source Patterns

## 3.1 Genspark Pattern

Mission Control uses Genspark-style thinking for: Tier 1 hero systems, fast visual mockups, initial landing-page polish.

The useful pattern:
```
Emotional promise + clean explanation + beautiful visual + obvious CTA + trust proof
```

The improved Mission Control version:
```
Premium hero + real customer pain + local trust + lead path + mobile-first CTA + sales package
```

## 3.2 PeachWeb Pattern

Mission Control borrows the premium perception layer. Use for: Tier 3 hero experiences, Tier 4 immersive builds, scroll-story sections, premium showcase pages, before/after proof.

## 3.3 Spline Pattern

Mission Control uses Spline-style thinking for: Tier 3 3D hero centerpiece, Tier 4 interactive visuals, equipment-focused businesses, contractors needing premium brand identity.

Examples by industry:
- Roofing: 3D home/roof shield
- HVAC: 3D unit with airflow
- Survey: 3D scanner/tripod on topo map
- Trucking: 3D truck/fleet route scene
- Plumbing: 3D pipe/water flow
- Concrete: 3D slab/finish system
- Assisted living: 3D warm home/floor plan

## 3.4 Unicorn Studio Pattern

Mission Control uses Unicorn-style thinking for: Tier 2 signature motion, Tier 3 visual polish, animated hero backgrounds, light motion without full 3D complexity.

## 3.5 Dora Pattern

The lesson: The user should describe or drop the business once. Mission Control should generate the first serious direction automatically.

## 3.6 Vectary Pattern

Mission Control uses Vectary-style thinking for: Tier 4 premium builds, equipment companies, product/service selectors, before/after transformations.

Tier 4 use cases: concrete finish selector, cabinet/furniture material selector, assisted living room preview, fleet showcase, survey service map, land clearing before/after interaction.

## 3.7 Webflow Pattern

Mission Control uses Webflow-style thinking for: reusable sections, service pages, location pages, review blocks, case studies, galleries, lead forms, schema, client editing logic, hosting/deploy discipline.

The lesson: Pretty pages need a real business website structure behind them.

## 3.8 Rosebud / SEELE Pattern

Mission Control uses this cautiously for: Tier 4 only, experimental premium visuals, interactive 3D demos.

Guardrails: Always use mobile fallback. Never let 3D block CTA. Never let WebGL kill speed. Never use 3D just to look cool.

# 6. Premium Hero Section Formula

Every hero must answer:
- What business is this?
- Who do they help?
- What problem do they solve?
- Why should the visitor trust them?
- What should the visitor click or call?

Standard hero structure:

**Left side:** Badge line · Strong headline · Short subheadline · Primary CTA · Secondary CTA · Trust proof line

**Right side:** Premium hero visual · Business-specific image · 3D-style object · Layered visual cards · Before/after preview · Equipment/service visual

**Background:** Controlled gradient · Soft glow · Depth layers · Subtle texture · No loud distractions

**Below hero:** Trust strip · Reviews/stats · Services · Problem/solution · Quote form or call CTA

The hero must pass the 5-second test.

# 7. Hero Composition Systems

## Layout A: Left Copy / Right Visual
Best for: Most Tier 1 and Tier 2 local businesses.
Rules: Left side carries conversion. Right side carries premium perception. CTA must be visible above fold.

## Layout B: Split Editorial Hero
Best for: Assisted living, remodelers, construction, land clearing, survey companies.
Rules: Strong typography. Editorial image crop. Premium lighting. Trust proof below headline. CTA remains visible.

## Layout C: Layered Visual Hero
Best for: Tier 2 and Tier 3.
Rules: Main visual plus floating cards (max 2-4). Cards must show proof or services. No decorative fake UI.

Good card content: Licensed & Insured · Free Inspection · 24/7 Emergency Service · 4.9 Star Rating · Serving Cleveland & Surrounding Areas · 25+ Years Experience · Same-Day Service · Fast Quote

## Layout D: Cinematic Full-Width Hero
Best for: Tier 3 and Tier 4.
Rules: Background visual spans full width. Overlay protects readability. CTA must remain obvious. Strong contrast required.

## Layout E: 3D Object Hero
Best for: Tier 3 and Tier 4.
Rules: One strong 3D centerpiece. Subject relates to industry. Open negative space for headline. No clutter. Static fallback required.

# 10. Lighting Recipes

## Soft Rim Light
Use for: 3D hero objects, equipment, vehicles, tools, homes.
How: Subject has soft rim highlight. Background slightly darker. Edges glow subtly.
Best tiers: Tier 2, Tier 3, Tier 4

## Warm Window Glow
Use for: Assisted living, home services, remodeling, residential services.
How: Warm interior glow. Cooler exterior. Creates safety and comfort.
Best tiers: Tier 1, Tier 2, Tier 3

## Storm Break Lighting
Use for: Roofing, restoration, emergency repair, tree service.
How: Dark clouds/background. Warm light breaking through. Business appears as the solution.
Best tiers: Tier 2, Tier 3

## Clean Studio Product Light
Use for: HVAC, plumbing parts, tools, trucks, machinery, concrete finishes.
How: Object on clean background. Soft reflection. Controlled shadows. Premium product feel.
Best tiers: Tier 1, Tier 2, Tier 3

## Technical Glow
Use for: Survey, engineering, logistics, map-based services.
How: Low-intensity cyan/green/blue glow. Grid lines or map lines. No neon overload.
Best tiers: Tier 2, Tier 3, Tier 4

## Golden Hour Contractor Light
Use for: Landscaping, concrete, land clearing, outdoor construction.
How: Warm sunlight. Long soft shadows. Textured ground/material. Strong before/after mood.
Best tiers: Tier 1, Tier 2, Tier 3

# 11. Depth Layering System

Layer 1: Background gradient or environment.
Layer 2: Soft glow, texture, map grid, or atmospheric depth.
Layer 3: Main subject.
Layer 4: Floating proof/service cards.
Layer 5: Subtle motion/light effect.
Layer 6: Foreground CTA and copy.

Rules:
- Copy must always stay readable.
- Use only 2-4 floating cards maximum.
- Floating cards must communicate real proof.
- Subject should not block CTA.

---

# 12. Glassmorphism Rules

Use: soft blur · thin border · low opacity · clear contrast · real content inside card

Good use: trust cards · service cards · review cards · quote callout · inspection offer · route/service area card

Avoid: too much transparency · low readability · 10 floating panels · fake dashboard clutter · purple neon glass panels · cards with meaningless icons

---

# 13. 3D-Style Visual Rules

Tier 1: cinematic rendered hero image · isometric service visual · floating depth cards · layered shadows · soft parallax-lite

Tier 2: animated 3D-style hero image · floating motion cards · subtle object drift · animated gradient/service effect

Tier 3: Spline-style 3D object · interactive hero centerpiece · mouse-follow effect · static fallback

Tier 4: WebGL/Three.js · product/service configurator · interactive 3D scene · CRM/automation/analytics integration

3D must support the business. Do not use 3D for decoration only.

# 14. Motion Taste Rules

Motion should: guide attention · make the site feel alive · support CTA · reinforce business type · feel smooth and intentional

Motion should not: delay comprehension · compete with headline · hide CTA · hurt mobile speed · create noise · use random stagger spam

Tier-specific motion rules: see doc 05-motion-rules.md

# 16. Typography Rules

## Industrial Premium
Best for: construction, concrete, trucking, survey, excavation.
Feel: strong, clean, technical.
Use: condensed or geometric headline font + readable body font.

## Warm Trust
Best for: assisted living, home care, family services.
Feel: human, warm, credible.
Use: soft serif or warm sans headline + clear body font.

## Clean Service Modern
Best for: plumbing, HVAC, electricians, roofers.
Feel: modern, reliable, approachable.
Use: bold sans headline + highly readable body font.

## Technical Precision
Best for: survey, engineering, logistics.
Feel: accurate, advanced, professional.
Use: structured sans, clear numbers, grid-friendly layout.

Rules: Headline must have character. Body must be readable. Button text must be clear. Avoid overly playful fonts for serious services. Avoid generic Inter unless approved.

# 22. Visual QA Checklist

## Hero
- Is the CTA visible above fold?
- Does the headline explain the outcome?
- Does the visual support the business?
- Does the hero look premium?
- Does it pass the 5-second test?
- Does it avoid generic AI design?

## Typography
- Is the font pairing intentional?
- Does the headline have character?
- Is body text readable?
- Are buttons clear?
- Is Inter avoided unless justified?

## Color
- Does the palette match the industry?
- Is contrast strong?
- Does the gradient feel premium?
- Is purple/blue AI neon avoided?
- Does the color system support trust?

## Motion
- Does motion guide attention?
- Is motion restrained?
- Is the CTA unaffected?
- Is mobile performance protected?
- Is stagger spam avoided?

## Visual Depth
- Is there background depth?
- Is the main visual clear?
- Are floating cards useful?
- Is glassmorphism controlled?
- Does the page feel expensive without clutter?

## Conversion
- Is phone/contact visible?
- Is trust proof near top?
- Is there a quote path?
- Is there a sticky mobile CTA?
- Does the design help the business get calls?
